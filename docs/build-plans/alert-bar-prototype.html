Alert run — over the map, between the panels

There is no bar. The chips float over the map, inset to exactly the span between the system drawer and the Tracker rail, so nothing reserves layout height and a flyout stays anchored under its own chip while never landing on either panel. The settings control is the last item in the run. Icons are the real lucide glyphs; chip height follows the icon size. Chips pack adaptively — spaced while there is room, overlapping EU5-style once there isn't, each shadowed rightward with the leftmost on top and the hovered one raised clear. Narrow the browser window to watch the packing change; only at the very end does anything collapse into a +N.

Fault slash
The fault slash applies to three chips only — Colony dying, Build blocked and Industry idle — so toggling it changes at most two things in an ordinary galaxy. The strip below the frame shows them side by side, plain against slashed, at 48px and at the chip's own size. Discrete events are one chip, not one per kind: Events carries the count and its flyout carries the individual events. Border conflict stays out of it as its own critical chip.
The fault overlay is lucide's own convention, borrowed. Its -off icons (WheatOff, RouteOff) are the base glyph plus a diagonal line corner to corner. Three of our categories have no -off variant in the library (FactoryOff and ConstructionOff don't exist), so they get that same line drawn over the plain glyph — the subject icon keeps saying what the thing is, the slash says it has stopped. How the placement landed here

Chips inside the existing top bar was killed by arithmetic. Its usable span holds about five chips against sixteen categories, so it only works if the list is cut to roughly a third of what the design calls for — and that space is already promised to treasury readouts.

A full-width bar under the top bar was built first and then dropped. It works, but it reserves map height permanently — including on a quiet galaxy with nothing to say — and it made the chips' vertical space a property of the band rather than of the chips. Its own inner container was already inset to the span between the panels, which is the tell: the full-width shell was doing nothing the inset run wasn't.

What survives from it is the fixed inset. The run is bounded by the two panel widths whether or not a drawer is open, so the chips never reflow when the player clicks a system — that was the objection to floating the run in the first place, and pinning the inset answers it.

Still open

Unrest rising is the one weak glyph left after the overlay. It has no subject icon to slash — Megaphone is the strike and Flame belongs to asteroid_strike — so it falls back to a bare TrendingUp, which says something is climbing but not what. A second overlay type (a rising marker over a subject glyph) would fix it and would cost a second visual convention to learn.

What clicking an instance does is still per-category and undecided. Every row here behaves like a Tracker row — fly the map, open a tab. Some of these should open the system panel directly, and the opportunity categories could plausibly apply the decision in place.

Volume is unmeasured. The category counts in this prototype are invented. Several important-tier categories could be continuously true across dozens of systems in a real galaxy, which is the precise EU5 failure this design exists to avoid, and no tier list should be fixed before that is measured.
